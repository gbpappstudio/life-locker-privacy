Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 September 2026

Life Locker is a completely offline document vault for Android, designed to help individuals and families securely preserve important information, organize personal records, and pass essential documents to future generations.

This policy explains, in plain language, what happens to your information when you use the app.

The short version: nothing you put into Life Locker ever leaves your device unless you personally export a backup file and choose to share it.

Life Locker is designed to function entirely offline. The app does not contain the Android INTERNET permission and cannot connect to online services, cloud storage providers, analytics platforms, advertising networks, or developer servers. The developer does not collect, share, sell, transmit, or store any user data.

What we collect

Nothing. Life Locker has:

  • No user accounts
  • No analytics
  • No crash reporting
  • No advertising
  • No tracking technologies
  • No developer servers

Because the app is built without the Android INTERNET permission, it is technically incapable of transmitting information off your device over a network.

Features and stored information

Depending on which features you use, Life Locker may store:

  • Documents and scanned documents
  • Photos and images
  • Family member profiles
  • Family tree records
  • Categories and folders
  • Tags and other metadata
  • Asset information and declared values
  • Expiry dates
  • Notes and checklists
  • Standalone reminders (birthdays, anniversaries, and one-off dates)
  • Saved logins (site name, username, password, an optional transaction password and PIN, URL, and notes) for the optional Password Manager
  • Application preferences, such as your chosen theme

All of it remains encrypted and stored only on your device, inside Life Locker's private app storage.

How your information is protected

  1. The database is encrypted with SQLCipher (AES-256).
  2. Every imported file is individually encrypted with AES-256-GCM, under a key held in the Android hardware-backed Keystore whenever the device supports it.
  3. Access to the vault is protected by a PIN you choose, stored only as a salted PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hash and never in plain text.
  4. Optional biometric authentication may be used for convenience, subject to your device's own security capabilities.

All encryption and security operations happen locally on your device.

Person-based vault

Life Locker organizes information around the people it belongs to, rather than around folders alone. You may create a profile for each family member and file documents, photos, assets and expiry-tracked records under that profile. Profile information is encrypted and stored only on your device.

Folders and categories

Life Locker supports categories and nested folders for organizing your vault. Folder structures, category names, and related metadata stay inside your encrypted vault and are never transmitted anywhere.

Document scanning

Life Locker includes a document-scanning feature for creating digital copies of paper documents. Scanning is initiated only when you choose to use it, and the resulting scan is protected by the same encryption as the rest of your vault. Life Locker does not upload scanned documents or store them anywhere but your device.

Scanning is handled by Google Play Services' own document-scanner component, which manages camera access in its own separate process — Life Locker itself does not request the Android camera permission directly.

Search

Life Locker can search across document titles, tags, categories, and people already in your vault, and across your notes, reminders, and saved logins (by site name, username, and address; never by the password itself). Every search runs live, entirely on your device, against your existing encrypted data — nothing is transmitted or shared.

Expiry tracking

You may record expiry dates for documents such as passports, licenses, insurance policies, and certificates. Expiry calculations and reminders are performed entirely on your device, and no expiry information is transmitted anywhere.

Notes

You may create text or checklist notes, optionally linking one to a person or a document already in your vault (for example, a note about where a physical original is kept). Notes are encrypted and stored only on your device. An optional one-time reminder for a note is scheduled and delivered entirely on-device, the same as expiry reminders below.

Reminders

Alongside expiry tracking, Life Locker lets you record standalone reminders for birthdays, anniversaries, and other one-off dates. These are stored and scheduled entirely on your device; no reminder information is transmitted anywhere.

Asset register

The Asset Register lets you keep records of valuables and important assets, including supporting documents and declared values. All of it is encrypted and stored only on your device.

Photo Bank

The Photo Bank stores versioned personal photographs for the three purposes it's built for:

  • Passport photo
  • Signature
  • Stamp-size photo

All Photo Bank images are encrypted and stored locally inside your vault.

Resize for upload (a Premium Trial feature — see below) lets you reframe a Photo Bank photo to a preset size for a specific visa or passport portal. All of its processing runs on your device. To place the head where the chosen size requires, it looks for the face in the photo when you open it; this uses Google Play Services' face-detection component, which Google Play downloads to your phone (as it does the document scanner above) and which runs on the device, so the photo is never sent anywhere. It also offers an optional "Remove background" tool that replaces a photo's background with a plain colour (white, light grey, or off-white, depending on which the selected portal preset allows) — this runs a subject-detection model that ships inside the app itself, and is off unless you turn it on. The resized image is saved back into your encrypted Photo Bank as a new entry; nothing is uploaded anywhere by this feature itself — you choose separately whether and where to share the result.

Password Manager and Autofill

Life Locker includes an optional Password Manager for saving logins (site name, username, password, an optional transaction password and PIN — useful for accounts like bank logins that split these into separate secrets, URL, and notes), a password generator, and integration with Android's own Autofill framework so a saved login can be offered when you sign in to another app or website.

  • Saved logins are stored in your encrypted, on-device vault, the same as every other kind of record in Life Locker, and are never transmitted anywhere.
  • Filling a saved password always requires a fresh PIN or biometric check at the moment of use — being unlocked elsewhere in the app, or the phone itself being unlocked, is never enough on its own.
  • Saving a new login you type into another app works the same way Android's own "Save password?" prompt does in any password manager, and stores it the same way as above.
  • Showing a saved password, transaction password, or PIN on screen, including while editing a login, also requires a PIN or biometric check.
  • Copying a password marks it as sensitive, so Android hides it from clipboard previews and keyboard clipboard history, and Life Locker clears it from the clipboard after 45 seconds unless you've copied something else since.
  • Autofill is off by default. You turn it on yourself, through Android's own system settings, and can turn it off the same way at any time.
  • Matching a saved login to the app or website asking for one happens entirely on-device, by comparing domains — no account, server, or network request is involved anywhere in this feature.

Family tree information

The Family Tree feature lets you record information about other individuals, including:

  • Names
  • Gender
  • Dates of birth
  • Dates of death
  • Relationships between family members
  • Optional photographs

This information is treated exactly like the rest of your vault: encrypted on your device, never transmitted anywhere, and never seen by the developer. It leaves your device only if you choose to export a backup and share it yourself. You can edit or delete any entry at any time from the Family Tree screen.

Because Life Locker operates entirely offline, the developer never receives, accesses, or stores any of this information. You are responsible for ensuring you have any permissions required under applicable law before storing information about other individuals.

Trash and recovery

When you delete an item, it's typically moved to Trash first and kept there for up to 30 days before permanent removal, so a deletion can be undone. Items in Trash remain encrypted and stored only on your device during that period. You can permanently delete anything from Trash at any time.

Backups

If you choose Settings → Backup & restore → Export backup, Life Locker writes a single password-protected .llbackup file, encrypted with AES-256-GCM, to a location you select. The encryption key is derived from your backup password with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 (600,000 rounds), so guessing the password is slow even for someone who has the file. If a backup can't be completed, Life Locker deletes the partial file rather than leave an incomplete copy behind.

Once exported, that file is under your control and subject to the privacy and security practices of wherever you choose to store or share it — cloud storage, messaging apps, email, or external storage. Life Locker cannot read the file back without the password you set, and neither can we.

PIN and backup password recovery

Life Locker cannot recover your PIN, your backup password, or your encrypted vault data. Encryption keys never leave your device, so there is no password-recovery service to offer. Please keep your PIN and any backup password somewhere safe.

Free trial for Password Manager and Resize for upload

These two features are free to use for a limited time from the first time you open Life Locker on your device, after which they fold into the same one-time purchase described below. To determine when that trial period began, Life Locker uses the earlier of two dates, both kept on your device:

  1. A timestamp stored in the app's own private storage.
  2. If you restore a .llbackup file, that backup's own recorded start date, so restoring your real vault on a new device doesn't unfairly reset the trial either.

None of this involves a server, an account, or any network request — it never leaves your device except inside a .llbackup file you yourself choose to export and share, in which case it's covered by the same protections described under Backups above.

Purchases

The optional one-time "Unlock Unlimited" purchase is processed entirely by Google Play Billing. Life Locker never receives or stores your credit card information, banking details, or payment credentials. Google's handling of the transaction itself is covered by the Google Payments Privacy Notice.

The only purchase-related information Life Locker stores is an encrypted Google Play purchase receipt, kept locally in the Android Keystore and used solely to confirm the unlock is genuine. It is never transmitted anywhere.

Permissions

Notifications (optional). Life Locker may ask permission to show notifications, used only for local, on-device reminders — for example, that a document in your vault is approaching its expiry date. On a locked screen these show only a general message, without the document's or reminder's name. No notification data is transmitted anywhere.

Autofill (optional, off by default). You may turn on Life Locker as an Autofill service through Android's own system settings, so it can offer a saved login in other apps — see Password Manager and Autofill above. Nothing about this requires a special Android permission, and it stays off until you turn it on yourself.

Not requested: internet access, location access, contacts access, camera access (see Document Scanning above), advertising identifiers, storage access, or background network access. Life Locker is designed to operate entirely offline.

Themes and preferences

Life Locker offers Light, Dark, and System theme options. Your choice, like every other app preference, is stored only on your device.

Children's privacy

Life Locker is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Data sharing

Life Locker does not collect, share, sell, rent, or transmit data to third parties. Everything stays on your device unless you explicitly export and share a backup file.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "last updated" date above changes with it. Because Life Locker is offline, a material change will generally be communicated through an app update.
